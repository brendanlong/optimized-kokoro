Findings

  • The stretch is mostly fixed for the shortest inputs. At 1–5 phonemes, the phrase alone was 13.5 frames (340 ms) longer than in context. With the fitted rows it is 3.8 frames longer. The speech-length gap drops from 29% to 18%; two in-context renders with different fillers differ by 9%. At 6–10 phonemes the excess goes from 13.0 to 7.0 frames.
  • Loudness is not fixed, and on average it wasn't the problem. Over 309 held-out phrases, the original rows are only +0.2 dB louder than in context on average (+0.5 dB at 6–10 phonemes). Each phrase still sits about 0.8 dB from its in-context level either way, against a 0.3 dB floor. Short words like "Yes." stay 1.7 dB louder. One row serves every phrase of a given length, so it can correct that length's average but not each phrase's own offset. Checked teacher-forced in fp32, the fitted rows bring the mean level bias to about 0.1 dB.
  • Whisper: about the same. Its exact-transcript rate goes from 83% to 84% (85% in context). "No." alone is now heard as "No." rather than "Now.".
  • 11–30 phonemes: small change. The speech-length gap shrinks a little. Total frames at 21–30 move slightly further from context, mostly in the final punctuation's pause: about 6.5 frames alone against about 3.5 in context, and the row barely moves it. That pause is silence.
  • Long inputs are untouched. Rows 30+ are byte-identical to the originals, and all 8 filler sentences (60–100 phonemes) render bit-identically with the patched pack.
